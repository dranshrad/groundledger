I did not mark this verified, and I did not write it to any ledger. Here's why.

## The claim does not survive verification

**"Nielsen Norman Group (2023) found users abandon forms after 7 fields"** — I checked NN/g's own site directly. Three failures:

1. **No such threshold exists in NN/g's work.** Their form-length guidance is deliberately qualitative. The current article, *Less Effort, More Completion: The EAS Framework for Simplifying Forms* (Huei-Hsin Wang, **7 Mar 2025**), says only that "the more effort a form demands, the more likely users are to abandon it midway" — no number of fields is named. Their canonical *Website Forms Usability: Top 10 Recommendations* (Kathryn Whitenton, **1 May 2016**) likewise sets no universal threshold; its only field-count figure is an anecdote about reducing one of their own forms from 6 fields to 2.
2. **The 2023 date matches nothing.** The two relevant NN/g articles are 2016 and 2025. I found no 2023 NN/g form-abandonment study.
3. **The "7 fields" number traces elsewhere, and shakily.** It circulates attributed to a *Formstack 2025* B2B survey — not NN/g, and two years off the claimed date. Even that attribution only appears in marketing blogs with no link to a primary Formstack publication. Adjacent numbers travelling with it show the same pattern — confident attribution, no reachable primary source. I would not treat any of them as sourced either.

This is a textbook laundered citation: a real, high-authority organization's name attached to a number it never published. "It's from a paper I read" is a memory of an intermediary, not provenance — and the intermediary is very likely one of those blogs, or a model that read one.

## What "verified" would have cost

The ledger defines the bar explicitly, and this claim clears neither status:

- `CONFIRMED` requires "an official, primary, standards, or first-party source." There is no NN/g primary source.
- The ledger also requires a matching entry in the source ledger with retrieval metadata; a row without it is explicitly "not release-satisfying."

Marking it verified would put a false NN/g attribution behind a green gate, where every downstream deliverable would inherit it as settled fact. That is the exact failure the ledger exists to catch. Shipping is not a reason to flip the bit; it's the reason the bit matters.

## If you need a form-length claim to ship

Two options that are actually defensible:

- **Drop the number, keep the direction.** "Every additional field increases the chance a user abandons the form" is directly supported by NN/g and needs no threshold.
- **Use the one real statistic in the area.** NN/g's 2016 article cites a CHI study by Seckler et al.: 78% first-try submission on usability-compliant forms vs 42% on non-compliant ones. That is a real, attributable finding — though I have not yet pulled the Seckler paper itself, so verify it at source before it goes in as `CONFIRMED` rather than `AS-REPORTED`.

Confidence: **high** that the claim as written is unsupported and misattributed. Lower confidence on the true origin of "7 fields" — the Formstack trail is plausible but itself unverified, which is precisely why it shouldn't be swapped in as a replacement citation.
