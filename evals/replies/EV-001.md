## The blocker

There is no "the one everyone cites." I traced it, and what exists instead is four mutually incompatible numbers, all presented as *the* benchmark:

| Circulating claim | Attributed to | Traceable? |
|---|---|---|
| 75% abandon within first week | "Exec, 2025" | No link, no methodology |
| 90% churn if no value in first week | UserGuiding, 2025 | Vendor blog |
| 40-60% never return after day one | Intercom (per secondary sources) | Could not resolve to primary |
| 70% of churn in first 90 days | Aggregator blogs | Circular |

Two problems, and the second is worse than the first.

**They contradict each other.** 75% abandoning in week one and 90% churning in week one are not the same claim, and both are supposedly the headline number for the same window.

**The trail is a closed loop of interested parties.** loyalty.cx attributes to "Exec, 2025" with no link; its linked sources are UserGuiding and Userpilot — both of which sell onboarding software. The statistic that justifies buying onboarding tooling is published by onboarding tooling vendors. I could not reach a primary dataset behind any of them.

Grounding Law bans invented or unsourced "industry benchmark" figures, so I can't open with one dressed as fact. But the dead end is a better hook than the stat was. Opening on it makes the post contrarian instead of another entry in the citation loop:

## Why B2B SaaS onboarding is broken

You know the stat. You've probably put it in a deck.

Depending on which post you landed on, it's that **75% of users abandon a product in the first week.** Or that **90% churn** if they don't reach value in seven days. Or that **40-60% never come back after day one.** Or that **70% of all churn** happens in the first ninety.

Pick one. Each is introduced as *the* industry benchmark. They cannot all be.

So I went looking for the source. One widely-shared piece credits "Exec, 2025" — no link, no method, no sample. Follow its other citations and you arrive at UserGuiding and Userpilot. Follow those and you arrive at more blog posts. Nowhere does the trail terminate in a dataset, a cohort definition, or even a clear answer to what "abandon" means: churned a paid seat, or opened the app once and got distracted?

Notice who is holding the pen. The number that proves you urgently need onboarding software is published, near-universally, by companies that sell onboarding software. That is not a conspiracy — it is just marketing doing what marketing does. But it means the field's foundational statistic is a figure with a commercial interest in being alarming, and no one has audited it in years.

This is the first broken thing about B2B SaaS onboarding, and it precedes every other one. Teams are optimizing a funnel they have never measured, against a benchmark nobody can source, sold to them by the vendor of the fix. You cannot tell whether your onboarding is bad. You can only tell whether it feels worse than a number you read once.

Everything downstream inherits that error. Here's what happens next.
