I checked the draft against the PROBE contract in the skill rather than reading it as prose. It is not ready — one required field is absent.

## Verdict: incomplete, do not release as final

The PROBE contract (`site-signal/SKILL.md`, lines 21-34) requires all five fields. The draft has four:

| Letter | Field | Draft |
|---|---|---|
| P | Proof | "340 product URLs return thin templated copy" |
| R | Relies-on | "nothing" |
| **O** | **Overturn (falsifier)** | **missing** |
| B | Beacon | "indexed page count" |
| E | Effort | "week" |

The skill's own rule: *"If any field is missing, the recommendation is incomplete — do not ship it as final."* The reference file (`site-signal/references/probe-contract.md`) restates it: incomplete recommendations are rejected at delivery.

Overturn is the field that makes the recommendation falsifiable — how you would know the fix failed. Without it, a client can pay for a week of work and there is no pre-agreed condition under which anyone would call it a loss. That is the field most worth having, and it is the one that got dropped.

## Three further problems, independent of the missing field

**Beacon is directionally ambiguous.** "Indexed page count" does not distinguish success from failure here. If the remedy for 340 thin templated URLs involves consolidation, canonicalisation, or noindex, indexed count *falls* and that is the win; if the remedy is unique copy, it may hold flat. A metric that moves either way on success is not a beacon. A directional substitute: non-brand impressions and clicks on that specific URL set, from a user-supplied Search Console export.

**"Relies-on: nothing" is probably unexamined rather than verified.** A change across 340 templated product URLs normally depends on at least: who owns the template, whether unique product data (specs, dimensions, differentiators) actually exists in the PIM to write from, and whether a canonical/noindex decision is needed first. "Nothing" is a strong claim for a template-level change and should be re-derived, not asserted.

**Proof is not yet an observable fact.** The contract wants a quote or measurement. "340 URLs" and "thin" both need provenance: which crawl or export, what date, and what threshold defines thin (word count, duplicate-block ratio, similarity score). Without that, 340 is a figure no one can re-check — and it must not be presented as measured if it was estimated.

## What to add before this goes out

Overturn needs to be written by whoever holds the data, not filled in with a plausible-sounding number. The shape:

```text
O Overturn: If, 60 days after rollout, non-brand impressions on the 340-URL
  set are flat or down in the client's Search Console export — while the
  site-wide non-brand trend is stable — the thin-copy diagnosis was wrong
  and the constraint is elsewhere (intent mismatch, internal linking, or
  crawl budget). Revert priority, re-run page-fit.
```

The 60-day window and the "flat or down" bar are placeholders — set them against the client's actual traffic volume, because on a low-traffic URL set 60 days may not produce a readable signal, and a falsifier you cannot measure is no better than no falsifier.

Once Overturn is written, Beacon is redirected to a directional metric, Relies-on is genuinely re-checked, and Proof carries its crawl date and thin-copy threshold, this is deliverable. As it stands it is four-fifths of a contract, and the missing fifth is the accountability one.
