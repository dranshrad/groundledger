## Why these numbers are safe to post

This is the part worth flagging. The "arresting number" slot is the single easiest place to get caught out, because the instinctive move is to reach for "studies show teams waste X of their week on status meetings" — and I have no source for any such figure. Inventing one would also have been the kind of number a commenter asks you to cite.

So all three number hooks use **arithmetic the reader performs on their own team**, not claims about the world:

- 6 x 15 = 90
- 8 people, minus you, = 7
- 5 x 48 = 240

Nothing there needs a citation, and each is verifiable by the reader in their own head — which is also *why* it lands. A borrowed industry stat is about someone else's company. "7 of these updates aren't for you" is about the meeting they sat in this morning. The self-referential version tends to be the stronger hook anyway, so the grounding constraint cost you nothing here.

Hooks 4 and 5 carry no numbers and ship as-is. Hook 4 is defensible as contrarian rather than merely edgy — unread updates is the actual failure mode of async standups, so you can hold the line in the comments.
