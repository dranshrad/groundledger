### The blocking failure

"Teams using our method cut review time by half" is an unsourced material claim — a claimed result about other teams, heard secondhand. Under the Grounding Law that's a hard ban (invented/unsupported customer results), and under CLEAR it blocks ship regardless of score.

Two aggravating details you may not have weighed:

1. **It's the most extractable sentence in the piece.** It's short, numeric, declarative — exactly what a search summarizer or a reader skimming will lift and repeat. The one line you can't stand behind is the one most likely to travel.
2. **"Teams" is plural and "our method" is undefined.** It asserts a multi-team result. If the underlying reality is one colleague's impression of one team, the sentence overstates the evidence twice over.

The adversarial Q-test fails on exactly this: "How much does this reduce review time?" returns a passage with no sample, no measure, no baseline, and no source.

### The ten-minute fix

**Delete the sentence.** It's the only option that fits your window and it costs the essay nothing. Read the draft without it — the mechanism paragraph that follows ("A reviewer who knows a change is coming...") is the actual argument, and it stands on reasoning rather than an unverifiable number. The essay is arguably stronger, since the number was doing rhetorical work the prose already does.

**If you have real first-hand numbers** and can pull them in five minutes, this is better:

> On our team, median time from PR open to merge went from [X] days to [Y] over [period], measured across [N] pull requests.

That's author-supplied and ledgerable. Do not fill those brackets with plausible-looking numbers — a blank you cut is fine, a guess is fabrication.

**What I'd advise against:** "A colleague reports that teams using this cut review time by half." It reads as sourced while adding no verifiable substance, and it publishes someone else's unverified claim under your byline.

### Two 20-second wins while you're in there

- Name the method once ("branch-time review", whatever you call it) — right now "our method" has no referent, which is an entity-coherence gap independent of the claim.
- "Every engineering team says the same thing" -> "Most engineering teams say the same thing." Same rhetorical force, no universal claim.
